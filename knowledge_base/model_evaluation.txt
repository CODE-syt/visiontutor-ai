A Confusion Matrix summarizes classification performance by cross-tabulating
predicted labels against actual labels, yielding true positives, false
positives, true negatives, and false negatives. From these, common metrics
are derived: Accuracy = (TP+TN)/(TP+TN+FP+FN), Precision = TP/(TP+FP),
Recall (Sensitivity) = TP/(TP+FN), and F1-score, the harmonic mean of
precision and recall = 2*(Precision*Recall)/(Precision+Recall). Overfitting
occurs when a model learns noise or idiosyncrasies of the training data and
fails to generalize to unseen data, typically indicated by a large gap
between training and validation performance; common remedies include
regularization, more training data, simpler models, dropout, and early
stopping. Underfitting occurs when a model is too simple to capture the
underlying pattern in the data, indicated by poor performance on both
training and validation sets, and is typically addressed by increasing model
capacity or reducing regularization.
