Linear Regression models the relationship between a dependent variable y and
one or more independent variables X by fitting a linear equation:
y = w1*x1 + w2*x2 + ... + wn*xn + b. Parameters are typically learned by
minimizing the mean squared error (MSE) between predicted and actual values,
using either the closed-form normal equation or iterative optimization such
as gradient descent. Logistic Regression extends this idea to classification
by passing the linear combination through a sigmoid function to produce a
probability between 0 and 1, and is trained by minimizing binary cross-entropy
loss (log loss). Key assumptions of linear regression include linearity,
independence of errors, homoscedasticity, and limited multicollinearity among
features. Regularization techniques such as L1 (Lasso) and L2 (Ridge) penalize
large coefficients to reduce overfitting.
