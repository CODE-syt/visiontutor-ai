Decision Trees are supervised learning models used for both classification and
regression. A decision tree splits the dataset repeatedly based on feature
values, choosing the split that best separates the classes or reduces
variance, using criteria such as Gini impurity, entropy (information gain),
or mean squared error reduction. Each internal node represents a test on a
feature, each branch represents the outcome of that test, and each leaf node
represents a class label or predicted value. Decision trees are easy to
interpret and require little data preprocessing, but they are prone to
overfitting, especially with deep trees, and are sensitive to small changes
in the training data (high variance). Ensemble methods such as Random Forest
and Gradient Boosted Trees combine many decision trees to reduce this
variance and improve generalization.

Random Forest builds many decision trees on bootstrapped samples of the
training data, each considering a random subset of features at every split,
and aggregates their predictions by majority vote (classification) or
averaging (regression). This reduces overfitting compared to a single deep
tree while retaining much of its interpretability advantage through feature
importance scores.
